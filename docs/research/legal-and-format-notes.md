# Anchoring Suraag's verifier in Indian evidence law

Suraag's rules can rest on a firm legal skeleton, a set of international handling norms and a reverse-engineered file grammar, but several load-bearing questions are unsettled and the report flags each one. The legal core is **s.63(4) of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023**, in force 1 July 2024. It requires a Schedule certificate with Part A (the party or person in control) and Part B (an expert). Both parts carry a hash value, a ticked algorithm (SHA1, SHA256, MD5 or "Other (legally acceptable standard)") and an enclosed hash report. The Supreme Court declined to strike it down at admission stage on 22 May 2026. No source names a mandatory hash algorithm, and the Court left open who may sign Part B. The international norms (ISO/IEC 27037, NIST SP 800-86, SWGDE, ACPO, Council of Europe) agree on copies, audit trails, documentation before interaction and recorded transfers, and they conflict on whether to power a seized phone off. WhatsApp export grammar, Indian CDR layouts and EXIF editing indicators are inferred from third-party parsers, one academic paper and forensic blogs, not from any official specification. The generation literature supports a hybrid design: the LLM writes narrative while code fixes times, counts and identifiers, because LLMs are weakest at date arithmetic and long-range consistency. Two cautions apply throughout. The statutory text came from bare-act reproduction sites because India Code was not reachable, and the ISO standards were never read beyond their tables of contents.

A note on tags. The legal notes tag sources PRIMARY (official text or court order read directly), SECONDARY (legal publisher, bare-act reproduction, law firm or news) or WEAK (blog or opinion). Notes from other areas used descriptive labels such as "vendor blog", "summarised through a fetch tool" or "low-reliability blog". This report keeps whichever label the note used, and it adds tags only where the note's description makes the tier unambiguous.

## Section 63(4) fixes the certificate fields but not the hash format

**Statutory structure.** BSA s.63(1) deems "computer output" (printed, stored, recorded or copied electronic information) a document, admissible without production of the original if the section's conditions are met. s.63(2)(a)-(d) sets four conditions: regular use, regular feeding of information, proper operation, and faithful derivation. s.63(3) treats several devices or systems as one. s.63(4) says a certificate "shall be submitted along with the electronic record at each instance where it is being submitted for admission" ([AdvocateKhoj s.63](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/bharatiyaaakshya2023/63.php); [AapTaxLaw s.63](https://aaptaxlaw.com/bsa/63-bsa-admissibility-of-electronic-records-63-bsa-2023.html); SECONDARY bare-act reproductions, which differ by one typo). The BSA is Act 47 of 2023, and s.170(1) repeals the Evidence Act 1872. s.170(2) keeps matters pending on 1 July 2024 under the old Act ([AdvocateKhoj s.170](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/bharatiyaaakshya2023/170.php); SECONDARY). The Supreme Court's own order confirms that s.63(4) replaced s.65B ([Pune Bar Association order](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/05/27/pune-bar-association-v-union-of-india-676590.pdf); PRIMARY). The commencement notification number was not confirmed from a primary source.

**The Schedule.** The reproduced Gazette text ([AdvocateKhoj Schedule](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/bharatiyaaakshya2023/b.php); SECONDARY) gives Part A these fields:

- declarant identity and a solemn affirmation;
- a tick-box source (Computer/Storage Media, DVR, Mobile, Flash Drive, CD/DVD, Server, Cloud, Other);
- make, model, colour, serial number and "IMEI/UIN/UID/MAC/Cloud ID";
- declarations of lawful control, proper working and regular feeding;
- an Owned/Maintained/Managed/Operated tick;
- a hash statement ("HASH value/s ... is ____, obtained through the following algorithm: SHA1 / SHA256 / MD5 / Other ____ (Legally acceptable standard)") with "(Hash report to be enclosed with the certificate)";
- a signature block with date (DD/MM/YYYY), time in IST (24-hour) and place.

Part B repeats the identity, device and hash blocks, adds "Name, designation and signature", and omits the regular-use declarations. The form specifies no hex case, spacing, per-file versus combined layout or hash length. The notes also found no official list of "legally acceptable" algorithms. A practitioner guide (WEAK: [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/tech-savvy-evidence-system-step-by-step-guide-hash-function-value-electronic-evidence)) demonstrates MD5 and SHA-256 and asks the government to prescribe approved functions. That request implies none existed in August 2024.

**What the Supreme Court decided.** In *Pune Bar Association v. Union of India* (WP(C) 599 of 2026, 22 May 2026; CJI Surya Kant, Bagchi and Pancholi JJ.) the bench rejected an Article 32 challenge to s.63(4) and the Schedule without issuing notice. It said a hash is "synonymous with an 'electronic fingerprint'", that including it "ensures authenticity and integrity", and that Part B gives "an additional layer of authenticity" to secondary electronic evidence ([order](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/05/27/pune-bar-association-v-union-of-india-676590.pdf); PRIMARY). On who may sign Part B, the Madras High Court had read only s.39(2) and required an s.79A IT Act examiner. The Supreme Court read s.39(1) and s.39(2) together. A person with special skill in computer science and cyber forensics may sign Part B if the court is satisfied "on the basis of unimpeachable material". It added that the Madras finding "shall not be treated as a binding precedent" and "keep[s] the question of law open" (same order; PRIMARY). The order says nothing about any hash algorithm or format. It is an admission-stage order, so the safe description is "validity upheld, Part B eligibility open". Verdictum's headline that such an expert "is competent" overstates the order's "it is possible to hold" ([Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/supreme-court/qualification-section-39-bsa-not-restricted-section-79a-it-act-examiners-forensics-sign-part-b-certificate-1614860); SECONDARY, contradicted in tone by the PRIMARY order). MeitY told the Delhi High Court that **21 forensic labs** are notified under s.79A ([Delhi HC Circular 186](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/files/2026-07/notifications-and-practice-directions/circular_no._186_dated_08.07.2026.pdf); PRIMARY).

**The case-law line, all s.65B-era.** The line runs from *Navjot Sandhu* (2005, no certificate needed) to *Anvar P.V.* (2014, certificate mandatory for secondary electronic evidence) to *Shafhi Mohammad* (2018, relaxation) to *Arjun Panditrao Khotkar* (2020, *Anvar* restored, *Shafhi* overruled) ([Nishith Desai](https://www.nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/dispute-resolution-hotline/supreme-court-requirement-of-certificate-pre-requisite-for-producing-electronic-records-4334); [Khaitan & Co](https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Supreme-Court-clarifies-on-the-requirement-of-a-certificate-under-Section-65B-of-the-Evidence-Act-1872); SECONDARY, summaries only; the judgments were not read). *Arjun Panditrao* exempts a produced original device. *Sonu @ Amar* (2017) holds that an objection to a missing certificate must be raised when the document is marked ([SCC Online blog](https://scconline.com/blog/?p=143961); SECONDARY). *Pooranmal v. State of Rajasthan* (10 March 2026) held call detail records inadmissible without the certificate and refused to let a telecom nodal officer's oral evidence substitute ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/supreme-court/call-detail-records-not-admissible-with-s65-evidence-act-certificate-supreme-court-525988); SECONDARY, from the report, not the judgment). The same judgment discounted a forensic report for broken chain of custody. High Courts keep applying s.65B to WhatsApp, for example the Patna HC in *Jyoti Rani v. Nishant Kumar* ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/patna-high-court/patna-high-court-whatsapp-messages-inadmissible-section65b-certificate-545171); SECONDARY). The Andhra Pradesh HC accepted a party's self-certificate from the person in lawful control of the phone ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/andhra-pradesh-high-court/andhra-pradesh-high-court-section-65b-electronic-evidence-self-certificate-whatsapp-533986); SECONDARY).

**Other statutes.** BNSS s.105 requires audio-video recording of search and seizure, "preferably mobile phone", forwarded "without delay" to a Magistrate ([AapTaxLaw s.105](https://aaptaxlaw.com/bnss/105-bnss-recording-of-search-and-seizure-through-audio-video-electronic-means-105-bnss-2023.html); SECONDARY). s.94 allows production orders for electronic communications and devices "likely to contain digital evidence" ([AapTaxLaw s.94](https://aaptaxlaw.com/bnss/94-bnss-summons-to-produce-document-or-other-thing-94-bnss-2023.html); SECONDARY). s.176(3) requires a forensic expert to visit the scene for offences punishable by seven years or more, phased in State by State within five years ([iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/mandatory-forensic-investigation-bnss-section-176-3/); WEAK blog; [WritingLaw](https://www.writinglaw.com/section-176-bnss/); SECONDARY). For charge labels, BNS s.318(4) (old IPC 420) carries up to seven years, s.318(2) up to three, s.319 is cheating by personation, and s.316 is criminal breach of trust ([iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/section-420-bns-cheating-318/); WEAK; [SCC Online blog](https://www.scconline.com/blog/?p=365805); SECONDARY). IT Act s.66C and s.66D each carry up to three years and a fine up to one lakh rupees ([UNODC SHERLOC](https://sherloc.unodc.org/cld/fr/legislation/ind/the_information_technology_act_2000/chapter_xi/sections_66c66d/sections_66c-66d.html); SECONDARY).

**What this means for rules, and what remains open.** Four rules follow from the sources. Treat a WhatsApp export, CDR CSV and JPEG as secondary electronic evidence needing a certificate and hash unless the scenario stipulates production of the original device. Carry the same hash and algorithm in Parts A and B, and flag a mismatch as an integrity defect. Model flawed variants (missing certificate, hash mismatch, algorithm not ticked, hash report not enclosed) as labelled defects. Do not hard-code "only s.79A examiners may sign Part B". These are the research notes' inferences and design choices, not statements of law. The gaps are as follows:

- No court has been found deciding whether s.63(4) applies to a primary electronic record. One LiveLaw author reads s.61 plus s.63 as ending the original-device exception ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/bharatiya-sakshya-adhiniyam-transformed-digital-evidence-552681); SECONDARY/opinion). *Arjun Panditrao* and the Bihar Police order take the opposite view.
- No Supreme Court decision applying s.63 BSA, as opposed to s.65B, to a concrete dispute was found.
- Photograph-specific and EXIF-specific case law was not found, and no Indian rule treats EXIF specially.
- BNSS sections on seizure power and preservation of devices (other than ss.94, 105 and 176(3)) are unconfirmed.
- Nothing after roughly June to July 2026 was exhaustively checked. A later review petition or State s.176(3) notification could have changed the position.

## International norms agree on process and split on power state, algorithm and what a hash proves

**Standards anchors.** ISO/IEC 27037:2012 guides identification, collection, acquisition and preservation of potential digital evidence, covers mobile phones and cameras, and was "last reviewed and confirmed in 2018" ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/44381.html)). Its clause headings (5.3 auditability, repeatability, reproducibility, justifiability; 6.1 chain of custody; Annex B minimum transfer documentation) come from the ANSI table of contents only ([ANSI preview](https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/INCITS/preview_INCITS+ISO+IEC+27037+2012+(R2019).pdf)). The notes never read any ISO body text, so Annex B's fields are unknown and rules should be worded "per ISO/IEC 27037 principles". The plain-language description of the four requirements comes from a vendor page ([TrueScreen](https://truescreen.io/fr/articles/iso-27037-digital-evidence-standard/)), not the standard. The widely quoted "relevance, reliability, sufficiency" principles could not be verified. Companions 27041, 27042, 27043 and 27050 rest on an unofficial ISO27k Forum site ([ISO27k](https://www.iso27001security.com/html/27041)), and 27050 edition status is unverified.

The more concrete sources were read as primary PDFs, but through summarising tools, so section numbers are indicative. NIST SP 800-86 requires comparing message digests of original and copy, a log of everyone with custody, and work only on copies ([NIST](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/SP/nistspecialpublication800-86.pdf)). SWGDE 18-F-003-2.0 (2025) requires documenting device state, identifiers and time before touching a phone, and a contemporaneous custody record with item ID, date and time, and name, title and signature at each transfer ([SWGDE](https://www.swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-08-21-Best-Practices-for-Mobile-Device-Evidence-Collection-Preservation-Handling-and-Acquisition-18-F-003-2.0.pdf)). ACPO v5 (October 2011) gives four principles: do not change data, competence, audit trail, responsibility ([ACPO guide](https://training-on-eppo.era.int/EPPO/kiosk/pdf/ACPO_Good_Practice_Guide.pdf)). No NPCC successor was found. The Council of Europe guide adds seal checks and photographs on opening, and it says changes for chat apps should be made to the application, not to the message database ([CoE](https://rm.coe.int/090000168097d7cb)). The shared core is that originals are untouched, an audit trail exists, evidence is documented before handling and transfers are recorded.

**Conflict 1: phone power state.** ACPO v5 says to power a live phone off to isolate it and stop remote wipe, except where a Faraday environment is advised ([ACPO](https://training-on-eppo.era.int/EPPO/kiosk/pdf/ACPO_Good_Practice_Guide.pdf)). SWGDE says never rely on airplane mode alone, confirm radios are off or use RF shielding, keep powered-on devices on consistent external power to avoid an AFU-to-BFU transition, and keep powered-off devices off ([SWGDE](https://www.swgde.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/2025-08-21-Best-Practices-for-Mobile-Device-Evidence-Collection-Preservation-Handling-and-Acquisition-18-F-003-2.0.pdf)). Indian documents split. The Sikkim Judicial Academy deck says if a phone is on, do not power it off; use airplane mode, disable Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and data, and use a Faraday bag ([Sikkim deck](https://sikkimjudicialacademy.nic.in/sites/default/files/PPTs/Digital%20Crime%20Scene%20Investigation.pdf); author and date not stated). The UP SOP says flight mode and radios off if on, do not open apps, do not charge if off, and use a Faraday bag ([UP Police SOP](https://uppolice.gov.in/site/writereaddata/siteContent/TRAININGDIROCTORATE/202410211032196102Collective%20SOPs%20For%20Crime%20Scence%20Investigation.pdf); undated). The Bihar order says "if off remove the battery" ([Bihar Police order](https://police.bihar.gov.in/misc/Officer_Orders/65B_Police_order_En.pdf); undated, issuer inferred). The workable resolution, which is an inference of the notes, is to make documented network isolation the invariant and not to mandate on or off. Even that is not authoritative. SWGDE's own caution that airplane mode alone is insufficient sits uneasily with Indian documents that rely on flight mode plus a Faraday bag.

**Conflict 2: hash algorithm acceptance.** The Schedule offers SHA1, SHA256, MD5 or "Other", with none mandated (above). SWGDE's position paper keeps MD5 and SHA-1 acceptable for integrity checks and file identification, recommends moving to SHA-2/SHA-3, and requires SHA-2 or SHA-3 where a file match is relied on as evidence without inspection ([SWGDE position](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/swgde-position-on-the-use-of-md5-and-sha1-hash-algorithms-in-digital-and-multimedia-forensics/)). The Council of Europe lists MD5, SHA-1 and SHA-256 but says MD5 and SHA-1 "should never be used exclusively" ([CoE](https://rm.coe.int/090000168097d7cb)). NIST will stop using SHA-1 for cryptographic protection by 31 December 2030 ([NIST CSRC](https://csrc.nist.gov/news/2022/nist-transitioning-away-from-sha-1-for-all-apps)). The Bihar order lists MD5, SHA-1 or SHA-256, and the Sikkim deck lists MD5 or SHA-256. So Indian law and police practice accept the weaker algorithms while the international bodies discourage them. A rule that prefers SHA-256 and flags MD5 or SHA-1 used alone is therefore stricter than Indian law, and the verifier should present it as good practice, not legal compulsion. SHA-256 is 64 hex characters, SHA-1 40 and MD5 32, so length checks are mechanical.

**Conflict 3: what a hash establishes.** The Supreme Court speaks of "authenticity and integrity". SWGDE 06-F-001 treats hashing as addressing data integrity only, with contamination and handling covered separately ([SWGDE 06-F-001](https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/06-f-001-swgde-data-integrity-within-computer-forensics/); an older document, currency unsure). The Bihar order says the certificate "addresses admissibility, not authenticity". The notes found no single authoritative sentence stating "a hash proves integrity, not authenticity". That statement is their synthesis, and the report keeps it labelled as synthesis. A SWGDE detail matters for the verifier: hashes from two separate extractions of the same phone may differ because mobile storage is dynamic, so a mismatch between extractions is not tampering. Compare an image against its own verification hash.

**Conflict 4: where the hash sits.** Bar & Bench and SupremeToday ([SupremeToday](https://supremetoday.ai/bharatiya-sakshya-adhiniyam-mandates-certificate-for-all-electronic-evidence-stricter-than-iea-20261003011); WEAK) place the hash in Part B. The Supreme Court order mentions hash for Part A, and the reproduced Schedule has hash blocks in both. The Schedule text and the PRIMARY order outrank the WEAK sources.

**Chain of custody.** The notes found no authoritative source that lists "seal number" or "hash value" as mandatory chain-of-custody columns, and Annex B was unreadable. The Indian SOPs (UP, Bihar, Sikkim) converge on the following:

- exhibit, FIR and police-station identifiers;
- collector, date, time and place;
- signed giver and receiver at each transfer with no time gaps;
- tamper-tape signed by the investigating officer and witnesses;
- the route scene to Malkhana to constable in transit to FSL, with an Evidence Submission Form and a seal sample.

Continuity checks are the notes' inference: receiver equals next giver, timestamps do not decrease, and the seal number at opening equals the one at sealing. The notes found no authoritative national SOP from MHA, BPR&D, NFSU, CERT-In or I4C. Their absence reflects the search limit, not proof of non-existence. State documents are undated or of unclear authorship. A CBI manual (2020) and the CBDT manual (2014) are known only through an NFSU paper ([NFSU Journal of Forensic Justice](https://jfj.nfsu.ac.in/Uploads/EJournal/4/7/(32-44)%20BYTES%20AND%20RIGHTS%20UNPACKING%20SEARCH%20AND%20SEIZURE%20OF%20THE%20ELECTRONIC%20EVIDENCE%20UNDER%20INDIA%E2%80%99S%20LEGAL%20FRAMEWORK.pdf)). A Delhi High Court order of 20 March 2025 directed hash-verified copying of seized phone data and an SOP from CBIC ([Delhi HC order](https://delhihighcourt.nic.in/app/showFileJudgment/68920032025CW115182024_113532.pdf); PRIMARY).

## File formats are reverse-engineered, so the verifier should accept a family of grammars

**WhatsApp exports.** There is no official WhatsApp export specification, and every grammar below is inferred from open-source parsers. WhatsApp's own help page says only that export yields a text file, with or without media, that cannot be re-imported ([WhatsApp FAQ](https://whatsapp.com/faq/en/android/23756533)). Android lines look like `15/03/2025, 9:41 pm - Asha Verma: text`: no brackets, a " - " separator, usually no seconds. iOS lines look like `[15/03/25, 21:41:07] Asha Verma: text`: brackets, seconds, often a leading U+200E mark. Date order, 12 or 24-hour clock, am/pm styling and separators follow the phone locale ([whatstk](https://whatstk.readthedocs.io/en/v0.7.0/_modules/whatstk/whatsapp/parser.html); [chatpack](https://docs.rs/crate/chatpack/0.6.0/source/src/parsing/whatsapp.rs); [whatsapp-chat-parser](https://unpkg.com/whatsapp-chat-parser@4.0.2/README.md); [WhatsR](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/WhatsR/html/parse_android.html)). The notes recommend whatsapp-chat-parser's header regex as the conservative single pattern for the verifier. Day-first versus month-first cannot be read from a file, and parsers default to days-first when inference fails. Continuation lines carry no header. System lines have no sender. The English encryption notice is "Messages and calls are end-to-end encrypted. No one outside of this chat, not even WhatsApp, can read or listen to them." ([LangChain loader mirror](https://huggingface.co/spaces/zhangyi617/webui/blame/main/langchain/chat_loaders/whatsapp.py)). Media appears as `<Media omitted>`, `IMG-20250315-WA0007.jpg (file attached)` on Android, or `<attached: 00000012-PHOTO-2025-03-15-21-43-12.jpg>` on iOS. Gaps stated in the notes:

- no changelog by app version;
- no en-IN-specific output (whether Indian locales use lower-case am/pm or four-digit years);
- no source for the edited-message marker;
- a U+202F narrow no-break space before am/pm is an unverified possibility;
- the 40,000-message and 10,000-media caps come from a vendor blog only;
- iOS "omitted" wording is only partly confirmed.

**What an export proves.** The only evidence on tamper-resistance is a 2026 review in a lower-tier journal, read through a summarising tool. It reports that the export has no signatures or embedded checksums, so timestamps and sender names can be edited invisibly. It calls physical acquisition the "gold standard" and names the on-device SQLite database as the main integrity source ([IJERT review](https://www.ijert.org/research/evidence-from-whatsapp-conversations-under-manipulation-a-systematic-review-of-forensic-techniques-c-IJERTV15IS061193.pdf)). A practitioner blog (WEAK) adds that iOS databases use Core Data timestamps counted from 2001, so mixed time bases can look like tampering ([Andrea Fortuna](https://andreafortuna.org/2026/07/14/whatsapp-forensics-2026/)). No standard, SWGDE document, NIST publication or Indian SOP addresses "Export chat" specifically. The notes' provenance ladder is an inference: device extraction with hash and tool log is strong, backup plus key is moderate, and an in-app export or screenshot alone is weak and should be flagged "unverified authenticity". A rule must never let a hash of an export imply the chat is authentic.

**Call logs and CDRs.** Two artefacts need separate models. The Android handset log is documented in AOSP: `number`, `date` in epoch milliseconds, `duration` in seconds, and `type` 1 to 7 (incoming, outgoing, missed, voicemail, rejected, blocked, answered externally) ([AOSP CallLog.java](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/base/+/refs/heads/main/core/java/android/provider/CallLog.java?format=TEXT)). An operator CDR has no published national schema. The best source is an IIT Kanpur paper: target and other-party numbers, date "in varying formats by each service provider", time in 24-hour or AM/PM, duration usually in seconds, abbreviated call-type codes (example `SMT`), start and terminating cell ID, IMEI and IMSI, with location from a separate cell-ID chart ([IIT Kanpur](https://ccjr.cse.iitk.ac.in/opapers/Using%20Telecom%20for%20Covid%20Tracing.pdf)). Operator column names (Airtel `A_PARTY`/`B_PARTY`, Jio `CALLED_PARTY`/`CALLING_PARTY`) come from a low-reliability hobby blog. The call-type vocabulary MOC/MTC, the MCC 404/405 values and the 15-digit IMEI and IMSI structure are the notes' background knowledge, not sourced. No source gives time-zone handling in Indian CDRs. The notes' design rule is to declare the zone in a header block and to allow style variation between files but not within one. Retention is two years under licence terms, with a trial court noting 15 months ([Indian Kanoon](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/59539466/)).

**Fictional numbers.** The National Numbering Plan 2003 and TRAI's 2020 recommendations say mobile numbers occupy levels 9, 8, 7 and some of 6, while level 5 is allocated to fixed-line services in some service areas and to short codes ([NNP 2003](https://dotws.cdot.in/sites/default/files/nnp2003_0_0.pdf?download=1); [TRAI 2020](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-09/Recommendations_29052020.pdf)). So `+91 5xxxx xxxxx` is not a mobile range, but it is not guaranteed unassigned, because a 5-leading ten-digit number can match a real landline (STD codes such as 0522 and 0542 exist). No official reserved fictional range, comparable to the UK drama numbers, was found. "Not found" is not proof of absence, and the later parts of both documents were not read. The notes advise a synthetic-data banner on every case.

**JPEG EXIF.** The standard is CIPA DC-008. Exif 2.32 dates from 2019 and Exif 3.0 from 2023. The OffsetTime tags arrived with 2.31, but the year is unresolved because Wikipedia gives both 2013 and 2016 ([Wikipedia: Exif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exif)). The primary CIPA document was not read. ExifTool documents the tag IDs (DateTimeOriginal 0x9003, OffsetTimeOriginal 0x9011, Software 0x0131, GPS IFD tags) ([ExifTool](https://exiftool.org/TagNames/EXIF.html); [GPS tags](https://exiftool.org/TagNames/GPS.html)). Date strings are naked local time (`YYYY:MM:DD HH:MM:SS`), and only OffsetTime carries a zone such as `+05:30`. Forensic guides name these editing indicators: a `Software` value naming an editor, a modification date later than capture, and mismatch with filesystem metadata. They also warn that EXIF is easily forged ([Forensic Focus](https://www.forensicfocus.com/articles/detecting-forged-altered-images/); [Belkasoft](https://belkasoft.com/photo-forensics)). The thumbnail-mismatch cue is well known but not found in any fetched source. Typical Make/Model/Software values for Indian-market phones are assumptions. Whether GPS time is UTC was not confirmed from a fetched source. A blog reports that WhatsApp standard-quality sends strip most EXIF (WEAK: [Fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/whatsapp-telegram-signal-photo-metadata-preservation/)). That yields a checkable rule: an `IMG-YYYYMMDD-WAnnnn.jpg` file should lack Make, Model and GPS unless sent as a document.

**Filenames and CSV.** WhatsApp names media `IMG-YYYYMMDD-WAnnnn.jpg`, using the receive or send date, as forum observation (not vendor documentation) ([Forensic Focus thread](https://www.forensicfocus.com/forums/general/whatsapp-media-file-naming-convention/)). Pixel phones moved from `IMG_` to `PXL_` names ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2020/08/20/google-camera-android-11-pixel-image-file-name/)). Samsung, Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and Realme patterns are unsourced, and the daily-reset behaviour of the WA counter rests on forum comments. Excel recognises UTF-8 reliably only with a byte-order mark ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/Excel/opening-csv-utf-8-files-correctly-in-excel)). Indian CDR deliveries vary in date style, so the notes recommend an ISO form or declared format metadata, and they treat a date as ambiguous when no day exceeds 12. No source covers Indian police CSV delimiter or encoding practice.

## UPI fraud typologies give plausible patterns but only secondary evidence of their artefacts

**Base rates and typologies.** For calendar 2025, MHA data reported by ThePrint show **28.15 lakh** cyber-fraud cases and at least **Rs 22,495 crore** lost. Investment fraud accounted for 76% of money lost (35% of cases), digital arrest 9% (6% of cases) and sextortion 4% (19% of cases) ([ThePrint, 21 Feb 2026](https://theprint.in/india/cybercrime-saw-24-spike-in-2025-indians-lost-rs-22495-crore-mainly-in-investment-scams/2859930)). The headline says 75% and the body 76%. A conflicting count of "over 24 lakh complaints" ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/over-24-lakh-cyber-complaints-22495-crore-fraud-reported-in-2025)) is probably definitional, and neither source explains the difference. Supreme Court records give digital-arrest complaints of 1,23,672 (2024), 58,239 (2025) and 16,377 to 30 June 2026 ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/sc-asks-rbi-to-frame-sop-for-handling-mule-accounts-orders-states-to-speed-up-cyber-victim-relief-enn26080406263)), which does not match 6% of 28.15 lakh. NCRB keeps no separate digital-arrest data ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2110809)). A 2025 PIB figure of 36.45 lakh was discarded because its unit was unconfirmed. The realistic weighting is therefore a few high-value investment cases, many low-value sextortion and "other" cases, and a smaller but heavily scrutinised digital-arrest category. The classic UPI tricks are older documented patterns:

- **Collect-request fraud.** NPCI directed that P2P collect stop from 1 October 2025 ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/08/npci-to-ends-p2p-collect-requests-on-upi-by-october-amid-fraud-concerns)). The NPCI circular itself was not read, so collect scenarios should be dated as legacy patterns.
- **Remote-access apps.** NPCI warned about AnyDesk-style apps in February 2019 ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/personal-finance-news/npci-cautions-consumers-against-downloading-anydesk-app-2886)).
- **SIM swap.** The sequence is documented by a bank awareness page ([Axis Bank](https://axisbank.com/fraud-awarness/sim-swap-fraud)).
- **Digital arrest.** MHA describes impersonation of police, NCB, CBI and RBI by "cross-border syndicates" ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/mha-alert-on-fraudsters-calling-citizens-as-cbi-ncb-officials-1503300277.html)). That source does not mention WhatsApp video, though 83,668 WhatsApp accounts were blocked for the purpose ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2110809)).
- **Task scams.** Police describe small early payouts followed by requests to "unlock" larger sums ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/cyber-police-warn-citizens-against-task-based-earning-scams-promising-easy-money)).

The RBI BE(A)WARE booklet could not be read, so fake-KYC and phishing-link typologies lack an official fetched source, and official per-typology UPI statistics were not found.

**Ring roles and tracing.** Delhi and Bengaluru police accounts describe handlers, coordinators, account providers, layered mule accounts and cash or USDT exit ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/delhi-police-busts-interstate-gangs-supplying-mule-bank-accounts-to-cybercrooks-9-arrested/2808326); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/mule-accounts-play-crucial-role-in-cyber-crime-bengaluru-police-2774243)). The Bengaluru case involved about 84 mule accounts, and private-bank data took six to ten days. Neither source defines the roles as an official taxonomy, so the operator, recruiter, mule, handler and cash-out labels are a synthesis. Tracing runs through the 1930 helpline and the cybercrime.gov.in portal into CFCFRMS, where a designated officer asks the bank to block the money ([PIB](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2110809); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/amp/story/retirement/cyber-fraud-rs-11158-crore-saved-across-328-million-complaints-how-to-report-financial-fraud-on-cybercrime-portal)). An MHA SOP dated 2 January 2026 limits holds to the disputed amount, requires banks to give police information within two calendar days, and sets 90-day hold reviews, though critics say it lacks statutory force ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/cfcfrms-reading-mha-new-account-freeze-sop-537503); SECONDARY analysis). The SOP's detail comes from a bank-hosted copy ([Zoroastrian Bank](https://www.zoroastrianbank.bank.in/download/SOP-NCRP.pdf)), and about 100,000 of 134,602 characters were read, so section numbers need checking against the primary PDF. The "golden hour" claim and the Rs 10 lakh e-Zero FIR threshold come from an unofficial blog only.

**Identifier realism.** The 12-digit numeric UPI reference (RRN, labelled UTR or UPI Ref No by different apps) is the cross-document key. NPCI replaced the YDDD date prefix with a STAN-based construction in April 2024, so newer RRNs should not encode a date ([Xflow](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/rrn-retrieval-reference-number); [Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/npci-revises-retrieval-reference-number-in-upi-to-avoid-duplicates-psps-and-banks-to-implement-revised-rrn-and-remove-validation-of-yddd-by-31st-may-2024/)). The notes treat the format as "confirmed by secondary sources", because the NPCI PDFs were blocked. App-specific long transaction ID prefixes, SMS alert wording and VPA handle specifics were not verified, and the notes warn against inventing prefixes as fact. The notes list the freezing SOP's per-notice fields: account number, UTR, amount and transaction date. A consistent evidence pack keeps those fields identical across the SMS, the app screen, the statement and the CDR rows.

**Anomalies and responsible design.** No official or academic catalogue of evidentiary anomalies was found. Two blogs mention metadata mismatches and edited screenshots, and the SOP describes officers analysing transaction timestamps against bank statements. The planted-defect ideas in the notes are labelled inference: a deletion instruction timestamped after the debit, a screenshot reference that differs from the statement's, an IMEI log that contradicts a "lost SIM" claim, and an app history missing an entry that the bank retains. On sensitivity, no Indian teaching guideline was found. The closest is an Australian government language guide, which advises against victim-blaming and stereotyping and recommends naming the deception method and including support options ([Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/system/files/language-guide-scams-communication-for-industry.pdf); publisher and date not confirmed). The notes' inferred design rules are:

- use invented names, banks and handles;
- name impersonated agencies generically;
- frame victims as targets of deliberate deception;
- point learners to 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in;
- avoid working scripts, live-looking phishing URLs and operational detail;
- mark all identifiers as synthetic.

## Prior art supports a hybrid design but offers no validated recipe for forensic chat generation

**Existing generators.** Every generator surveyed validates by construction, with recorded ground truth, and then by qualitative checks. Only EvidenceForge reports a quantitative `eval` score, and it covers security logs, not chat artefacts. EvidenceForge (Cisco Talos, MIT licence; author David Bianco, SANS-listed, so the "SANS" attribution is loose) keeps the LLM out of record generation. The AI co-develops a YAML scenario, while the engine makes no LLM calls and is seed-reproducible. It scores bundles on Parseability 30%, Plausibility 25%, Causality 25% and Timing 20% ([Talos blog](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/introducing-evidenceforge-synthetic-security-logs-that-dont-look-as-fake/); [GitHub README](https://github.com/THORCollective/EvidenceForge)). The only LLM-driven forensic scenario generator found is Plankl, Göbel and Baier (DFRWS EU 2026, *Digital Investigation* vol. 56, art. 302053). It chains prompts for personas, plot and activities into ForTrace configurations. Only GPT-5-mini reliably enforced prompt-stated coherence rules, models defaulted to their training-cutoff years (2023 for GPT-4o-mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash, 2025 for GPT-5-mini), and the paper recommends moving deterministic tasks to external functions. Its evidence is essentially one case study with no quantitative benchmark ([DFRWS PDF](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Addressing-the-dataset-gap-problem-with-Generati_2026_Forensic-Science-Inter.pdf)). The earlier line (hystck 2020, ForTrace 2022, EviPlant 2017, ForGe) uses two-scenario validations with reporter-generated ground truth. ForTrace's chat-app modules were "still planned" in 2022 ([ForTrace PDF](https://www.rz.unibw-muenchen.de/digfor/publikationen/pdf/2022-03-dfrws-eu-goebel.pdf)). A 2026 WDF paper builds a synthetic fraud dataset with SHA-256 manifests and automated checks of temporal coverage and transaction counts, and it aligns to ETSI CDR and the CASE ontology ([CEUR Vol-4329](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4329/paper3.pdf)). No generator-specific paper on LLM-generated forensic chat datasets was found. VeriScene (arXiv 2609.08342) is a physical crime-scene reconstruction tool, not a digital-forensics tool, and it is borrowed only for its cite-and-audit pattern ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.08342); preprint, peer-review status unknown).

**Verification of the four previously unverified claims.** The notes checked four claims, and the results should override any earlier memory:

| Claim | Finding |
|---|---|
| MuSR "62%" | MuSR is real (ICLR 2024). GPT-4 scored 80.4 on murder mysteries (CoT+), against 50.0 for chance and 94.1 for human majority. "62" matches GPT-3.5 (61.6, or 62.0 in a separate table) and an unrelated 62% fact-entailment figure ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16049)). |
| Test of Time "13-36%" | Test of Time is real (ICLR 2025). No such range is stated. The nearest figures are 13.5% on duration arithmetic and Timeline scores of 24-36% in one table ([ICLR PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/eb7295a8bc613b375726659c2ecd6f14-Paper-Conference.pdf); [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09170v1)). |
| "Plankl et al. 2026" | Real, as described above. |
| "Re-imagen" | A Google text-to-image paper (arXiv 2209.14491), unrelated to forensics. No forensic work by that name was found ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14491)). |

The Test of Time ICLR range for duration is much wider than "13-36%": ToT-Arithmetic by category runs from 13.50% (Gemini 1.5 Pro, Duration) to 92.00% (GPT-4o, Timezone), so the safe statement is that duration arithmetic is the weakest area, with only about 13.5% for the weakest model on Duration, while timezone conversion is much stronger. Only Tables 5 and 8 of MuSR were visible, so other GPT-4 settings were unchecked.

**LLM consistency and rule-based checking.** ConStory-Bench reports consistency errors growing roughly linearly with story length. Factual and timeline failures dominate, and absolute-time and geographic errors show the largest distance between the establishing fact and the contradiction. Error density ran from 0.113 per 10,000 words (GPT-5-Reasoning) to above 3.0 for some open models. Its LLM-as-judge checker reached recall of only 0.55 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05890)). ConWriter's static and dynamic memory with pre- and postcondition validation cut error density by roughly 50-87% in a small 20-case evaluation ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05169)). FactTrack tracks facts with validity intervals and flags contradictions when intervals overlap ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16347v2)). In forensics specifically, ChatGPT-4o mis-ordered events in Plaso timelines and did worse than a rule-based approach ([arXiv 2505.03100](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.03100v1)). The notes found no source measuring contradiction rates in LLM-generated chat or message logs. They also found no peer-reviewed rule-based consistency checking for forensic narratives, and the 2025 timeline-reconstruction SoK was not read. The candidate rule families are the notes' inferences: causal prerequisites, entity and identifier consistency across artefacts, temporal bounds and time zones, quantity reconciliation, fact-interval conflicts, answer-leak checks and recoverability of each ground-truth item from the emitted evidence. The last of these has no cited implementation. The notes call it a novel contribution, not established practice.

**Teaching-case design.** Göbel, Baier and Breitinger argue that "how realistic" is the wrong question and that synthetic data's value lies in ground truth, adaptability and seedable per-student cases. They propose no quality metrics ([paper PDF](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/117557/117557.pdf)). Wang, Bai and Goda report that over-specific clues make a case trivial and that undecryptable data is too hard ([SIGITE 2019](https://faculty.washington.edu/godab/Pubs/p33-wangA.pdf)). The case for per-student unique datasets is design rationale only, from Russell et al. and EviPlant. No empirical study of cheating or solution-sharing in forensic courses was found, and no source describes grading students on cited evidence identifiers.

## Where sources disagree and what Suraag cannot yet claim

| Issue | Position A | Position B | Handling in the notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone power state | ACPO v5: power off, with Faraday as the exception | SWGDE and Sikkim: keep state and isolate; Bihar: remove battery if off | Verify documented isolation, not on/off |
| Hash algorithm | Schedule: SHA1, SHA256, MD5 or Other; none mandated; Bihar and Sikkim accept MD5 | SWGDE and CoE: SHA-2/3 preferred; MD5/SHA-1 not alone; NIST retires SHA-1 by 2030 | Prefer SHA-256 as good practice, not law |
| Integrity vs authenticity | Supreme Court: hash serves "authenticity and integrity" | SWGDE: integrity only; Bihar order: certificate is "admissibility, not authenticity" | Report as two positions; never let a hash of an export imply authenticity |
| Hash in Part A or B | Schedule text: both; Court mentions Part A | Bar & Bench and SupremeToday: Part B | Schedule and PRIMARY order outrank WEAK sources |
| Part B signatory | Madras HC: s.79A examiner only | Supreme Court: wider via s.39(1), question open | Do not hard-code |
| Certificate for primary records | LiveLaw author: required for all, including originals | *Arjun Panditrao* and Bihar order: not needed if the original device is produced | Include a certificate in both scenarios |
| Complaint count 2025 | 28.15 lakh cases | "Over 24 lakh" NCRP complaints | Definitional, unexplained |
| Digital arrest volume | 6% of 28.15 lakh | 58,239 complaints in 2025 (SC record) | Definitions differ, unreconciled |
| Verdictum headline | Expert "is competent" | Order says "it is possible to hold" | PRIMARY order controls |

Several things remain "not found", and a generator should not assert them as fact. These are:

- an official hash algorithm list or format;
- a WhatsApp export specification;
- an Indian CDR schema;
- an Indian CDR time-zone convention;
- a reserved fictional Indian number range;
- thumbnail-mismatch support from a fetched source;
- Annex B text;
- an NPCC successor to ACPO;
- an Indian generation or teaching guideline for fraud scenarios;
- CFSL/RFSL official mobile or WhatsApp lab procedure;
- empirical data on cheating in forensic courses.

## Conclusion

The sources shift Suraag's problem from "what is legally required" to "what is merely good practice, and what is contested". The one hard rule is structural: a certificate with Parts A and B, a ticked algorithm, a hash value and an enclosed hash report. Almost everything else the verifier checks is stricter than the law, as with SHA-256 preference, or inferred from outside it, as with the WhatsApp grammar, the CDR layout and the EXIF editing signs. Each such rule should therefore be tagged by grounding strength: statute or court text, standards body, state SOP, or inference. The tags let a trainer see why a defect is flagged and let the team revise a rule when the law moves. That matters because the Part B question is explicitly open, the primary-record question is undecided, and the notes' coverage thins after mid-2026.

The prior art points to a clear gap and a clear design. No source validates LLM-generated forensic chat or message data, and none checks that each planted ground-truth item is recoverable from the emitted files. EvidenceForge and Plankl et al. both converge on code for concrete values and an LLM for narrative, and the LLM-consistency results show why. Absolute times, durations and counts are where models fail most, and an LLM judge catches only about half of injected contradictions. Suraag's verifier should therefore check structured ground truth with deterministic rules, and it should reserve the LLM for prose that must cite the fixed values. The remaining bet is that this combination is sufficient for forensic realism, and no published evidence yet confirms that.
