8. Plausible wrong suspect: Dev Malhotra

Dev is deliberately included as a misleading suspect.

Reasons students might suspect Dev:

Chat line 7: He notices the transaction-history entry.

Chat line 12: He mentions receiving a notification.

Chat line 20: He denies telling anyone to delete anything.

Call-log row 4: He calls Rohan at 09:40:15, shortly before Rohan confirms deletion at 09:41 (chat line 17).

However:

No supplied chat message directly shows Dev issuing the deletion instruction.

Kabir explicitly issues the instruction in lines 13 and 15.

Rohan directly attributes the instruction to Kabir in line 23.

Teaching point: Suspicious timing and technical knowledge are not equivalent to proof of responsibility.

10. Discussion questions for instructors

Can EXIF GPS metadata alone establish a person's physical location beyond reasonable doubt?

Why should investigators distinguish between a call's timestamp and the contents of that call?

Does deleting a screenshot necessarily delete the underlying bank transaction record?

How could investigators independently corroborate the device's clock and GPS location?

What additional evidence would be necessary before attributing criminal intent to Kabir?

Instructor's final forensic caution: This is a constructed teaching scenario, not a real criminal investigation. The exact-coordinate match is intentionally simplified for student exercises. In actual digital forensics, metadata authenticity, clock drift, GPS accuracy, device ownership, and chain of custody must all be examined before drawing conclusions.
