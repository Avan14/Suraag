1. Case background

On 21 November 2026, a university student reported that ₹18,750 had been fraudulently transferred from their UPI account.

The money was transferred to a mule account allegedly operated by a person named Rohan Mehta.

During the investigation, police obtained:

A WhatsApp-style group chat export.

A six-entry call log from a recovered mobile phone.

One photograph recovered from the device.

EXIF metadata associated with the photograph.

Three candidate locations connected with the investigation.

Investigators suspect that members of a small group coordinated the transfer, discussed deleting transaction evidence, and attempted to establish an alibi.

6. Student investigation questions

Question 1 — Instruction attribution

Who instructed Rohan to delete the UPI transfer record?

Question 2 — Timeline reconstruction

At what exact date and time was the deletion instruction given?

Question 3 — Geolocation analysis

Where was the mule (Rohan) most likely located when the photograph was taken?

7. Answer key
Instructor copy
Answer 1: Who instructed deletion?

Answer: Neha Sharma.

Supporting evidence:

Chat line 14 explicitly states: "Rohan, transfer record delete kar do."

Chat line 15 shows Rohan acknowledging the instruction.

Call-log row 3 records an outgoing call to Neha at 18:31:05, shortly before the deletion instruction.

Conclusion: Neha is the person explicitly identified in the recovered chat as giving the deletion instruction.

Answer 2: When was the instruction given?

Answer: 21 November 2026 at 18:40.

Supporting evidence:

Chat line 14: 21-11-2026, 18:40 — Neha gives the instruction.

Chat line 15: 21-11-2026, 18:42 — Rohan acknowledges it.

Call-log row 3: 18:31:05 — preceding communication with Neha.

Conclusion: The instruction was recorded at 18:40, with acknowledgment two minutes later.

Answer 3: Where was Rohan when the photo was taken?

Answer: Phool Bagh, Gwalior.

Supporting evidence:

Chat line 18: Rohan says he is outside and will send a photograph.

Chat line 20: Rohan says the photograph will provide a location clue.

EXIF DateTimeOriginal: 2026:11:21 19:02:18.

EXIF GPS: 26.205000, 78.170000.

Candidate B, Phool Bagh: 26.2050, 78.1700.

Conclusion: The photograph's GPS coordinates exactly match the supplied Phool Bagh candidate coordinates.

8. Deliberate misleading evidence: Wrong suspect

Sameer Khan is a plausible but incorrect suspect.

Reasons students might initially suspect him:

Line 9: He discusses the payment.

Line 16: He announces that he is leaving the group.

Line 22: He reports being questioned by police.

Call-log row 6: He calls Rohan at 19:09:17.

However:

No chat line explicitly shows Sameer instructing Rohan to delete the transfer record.

His call occurs after the 18:40 deletion instruction.

His statements do not establish that he controlled the mule account.

Instructor note: Students should not confuse suspicious behavior, association, or communication with proof of a specific action.

10. Discussion prompts

For a more advanced forensic exercise, ask students:

Can EXIF metadata alone establish the physical presence of Rohan?

Does a call-log entry prove what was discussed during the call?

What additional evidence would help establish whether the transaction record was actually deleted?

How would investigators distinguish original device evidence from edited or fabricated exports?

What chain-of-custody procedures should be followed before presenting these artifacts in court?

Teaching takeaway: Digital forensic investigations rely on correlation between independent evidence sources. A chat message, call record, and photograph become more meaningful when their timestamps and contents are consistent—but none should be treated as conclusive in isolation.
